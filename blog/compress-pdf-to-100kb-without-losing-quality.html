How to Compress Scanned PDFs to 100KB Without Losing Quality

Published on June 16, 2026 • 6 min read

Whether you are applying for a government job, submitting KYC documents to a bank, or uploading your Aadhar Card to a scholarship portal, you will almost certainly encounter a strict file size limit. The most common requirement? "Upload PDF file under 100KB."

However, when you scan a document using your phone, the resulting PDF is often 2MB to 5MB. Shrinking it down to just 100KB usually results in a blurry, unreadable mess that gets your application rejected. In this guide, we will show you exactly how to compress your PDFs correctly so the text remains sharp and legible.

1. Why is Scanned PDF Compression So Difficult?

PDFs come in two types: text-based and image-based (scanned). Text-based PDFs are naturally very small. Scanned PDFs, on the other hand, are essentially large photographs wrapped inside a PDF file. To reduce the size of a scanned PDF, the compressor must lower the resolution and quality of the images inside it. If done poorly, the text merges into a pixelated blur.

3. Pro-Tips for Retaining Readability

If you are compressing a massive 5MB file down to 100KB, some quality loss is inevitable. However, you can use these tricks to ensure the text remains perfectly readable:

  • Scan in Grayscale (Black & White): Color images hold a lot of data. If you are scanning a marksheet or text document, switch your scanner app setting to "B&W" or "Grayscale". A black-and-white PDF compresses much better and stays sharper at 100KB than a color one.
  • Crop the Empty Margins: If your document is sitting in the middle of a large white background, crop it out before creating the PDF. The less "empty space" the image has to process, the more data can be allocated to the actual text.
  • Keep it to One Page: If a portal asks for a 100KB limit, it generally expects a single-page document. Trying to squeeze a 5-page PDF into 100KB will result in 20KB per page, which is almost impossible to read.

4. Common Use Cases for 100KB PDFs

Here are a few places where this exact size limit is frequently enforced:

  • UPSC & SSC Exams: Identity proof uploads (Aadhar/Voter ID) are often capped at 100KB to 300KB.
  • Bank KYC Portals: When submitting PAN cards for account opening or updates.
  • University Admissions: Uploading 10th and 12th passing certificates or degree marksheets.
