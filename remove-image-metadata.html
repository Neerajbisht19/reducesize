Why You Should Remove EXIF Metadata from Photos

Whenever you take a photo with your smartphone or digital camera, the device invisibly embeds a chunk of text data inside the image file. This data is known as EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) metadata. While it seems harmless, sharing uncleaned photos online can expose severe privacy risks.

What is hidden inside your photos?

  • Exact GPS Coordinates: If your phone's location is on, your photo contains the exact latitude and longitude of where you were standing. Anyone can track it to your home or office address.
  • Camera & Phone Model: Information about the exact device (e.g., iPhone 15 Pro, Samsung Galaxy) you used.
  • Date & Timestamp: The exact second the photograph was captured.
  • Software Details: Tools used to edit the image, like Photoshop.

How our EXIF Stripper Works (100% Offline)

Our tool works fundamentally differently from other websites. When you select an image, our script reads the EXIF data directly inside your web browser and displays it to you. When you click "Scrub Data", we redraw your image entirely from scratch using HTML5 Canvas. This process preserves the visual quality of your photo but permanently destroys the hidden text layer. Because the file never leaves your device, your location data remains strictly yours.
