Compress Image to Exact KB — Free & Instant

ReduceSize.online is a free, browser-based tool that lets you compress images and PDFs to a precise file size — whether that's 10KB, 20KB, 50KB, or 100KB — directly inside your browser. Your files never leave your device, so there is no privacy risk, no waiting for uploads, and no size limits imposed by a server.

Government exam portals, job applications, and college admissions often reject files that exceed their size limits. Even 1KB over the cap means your form gets rejected. ReduceSize uses a smart binary-search compression algorithm to hit your exact target size every single time — no guessing, no manual retrying.

Why Exact KB Compression Matters

Most image compression tools reduce file size by a fixed percentage or to a rough estimate. That approach works fine for casual use, but it fails completely when you need to meet a strict file size requirement — like the ones on SSC, UPSC, Railway, or banking exam portals.

ReduceSize takes a different approach. It adjusts both image quality and dimensions together in a precise loop until your file lands exactly at or below the number you enter. The result is the highest possible image quality within your chosen size limit, delivered in seconds.

Who Uses This Tool

ReduceSize was originally built for students and job seekers in India who struggle with strict government portal requirements. Today it is used by a wide range of people:

  • Students filling SSC CGL, SSC CHSL, UPSC Civil Services, Railway NTPC, Police Constable, and other government exam forms that require photos under 20KB or 50KB.
  • Professionals sending email attachments with size caps — many email clients block files over 1MB, and compressing images beforehand prevents delivery failures.
  • Freelancers and designers who need to optimize images for websites, apps, or client presentations without sacrificing too much visual quality.
  • Anyone uploading passport photos, ID card scans, or signature images to government portals, banking sites, or e-commerce platforms that enforce strict upload limits.

How to Compress an Image to an Exact KB Size

  1. Select your file: Click the upload area or drag and drop one or more images. JPG, PNG, and WEBP formats are all supported. You can select multiple files at once.
  2. Set your target size: Use one of the quick-select buttons (10KB, 20KB, 30KB, 50KB, 100KB, 200KB) or type any custom value in the input box. Switch between KB and MB as needed.
  3. Compress and download: Click the Compress & Download button. The tool processes everything locally in your browser and delivers the compressed file in seconds. No upload, no waiting.

How the Compression Algorithm Works

When you click Compress, ReduceSize runs a binary-search loop that adjusts JPEG quality and image dimensions simultaneously. It starts at a mid-range quality setting, checks the output file size, then narrows the range — increasing quality if the file is too small, decreasing it if too large. After up to 25 iterations per image, it converges on the highest quality setting that keeps the file at or below your target. If quality alone cannot reach the target, the image dimensions are also scaled down proportionally.

This approach produces better results than simple quality sliders because it treats both quality and resolution as variables, finding the optimal combination for the exact size you need.

Privacy and Security

Many online tools upload your images to a remote server for processing. This creates privacy risks — your personal photos, ID scans, or passport images could be stored, logged, or accessed by third parties. ReduceSize processes everything entirely inside your web browser using JavaScript. Your files are never transmitted over the internet, and nothing is stored anywhere outside your own device. Once you close the browser tab, all data is gone.

Supported File Types

The image compressor supports JPEG, PNG, and WEBP input files and outputs optimized JPEG files. The PDF compressor works best with scanned or image-based PDFs — it re-renders each page at an adjusted quality level to meet your target size.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I resize an image to exactly 20KB?
Upload your image, type 20 in the target size field, make sure KB is selected, and click Compress & Download. The tool will output the highest-quality version of your image that fits within 20KB.

Does it work for SSC and UPSC government forms?
Yes — the tool was specifically built for this use case. The quick-select buttons cover the most common government exam size requirements: 10KB for signatures, 20KB for form photos, 50KB for larger portrait photos.

Can I compress multiple images at once?
Yes. Select as many images as you need in the file picker and all of them will be compressed to your target size in a single batch. Each file can be downloaded individually from the results grid.

Will my image quality suffer a lot?
Quality is maximized for the size you choose. At very small sizes such as below 15KB, some quality loss is unavoidable because there is simply less data available to represent the image. For most government form requirements (20KB–100KB), the output quality is clear enough for official use.

Is there a file size limit for uploads?
No server-side limit exists because your files never leave your browser. Very large source files (above 20MB) may take a few extra seconds to process depending on your device speed.

Does it work on mobile phones?
Yes. The tool is fully mobile-responsive and works on Android and iOS browsers. You can select images directly from your phone's camera roll and download the compressed output to your device.

What is the difference between KB and MB compression?
KB (kilobytes) is used for small files like form photos and signatures. MB (megabytes) is used for larger files like high-resolution photos or multi-page scanned documents. The unit selector lets you switch between both.

Can I compress a PDF to a specific KB size?
Yes. Switch to the PDF tab, upload your PDF, enter a target size in KB, and click Compress. The tool renders each page as an image and rebuilds the PDF at the compressed quality. This works best for scanned documents.
